# Dataset methodology

Version 1.0.0. Authored 2026-09-26. Five synthetic archetypes; no client records.

The selection deliberately varies owner count, financing, US footprint, existing business/IP, and travel plans. Geography gives narrative context, not a claim of country-specific legal coverage. These cases are not a statistically representative sample.

Each prompt includes an incomplete request and a misconception or unresolved decision. The provided-facts list must be traceable to that prompt. Missing facts are not filled by guesswork. Questions explain their purpose. Concerns cite supporting primary sources where a US legal proposition is made. Unresearched local-law issues remain evidence gaps.

The source registry contains consulted official government pages, a checked date, jurisdiction, a limited paraphrase, and conflict status. The application does not continuously refresh or verify these sources. Source applicability to an individual client requires human judgment.

Case questions are illustrative proposals, not historical questions asked of a client. Specialist handoffs identify both decisions and necessary evidence. No specialists have validated this draft dataset.

The structured intake uses seven explicit selections. Unknown and invalid input stay unknown; free text is retained without automatic extraction. Rules emit review tasks, never eligibility approval or tax conclusions. Case data is not changed by practice answers. Browser-memory notes disappear on reload; exports include user-entered notes.

Before adding real cases, obtain authorization, remove identifying and reidentifying details, maintain provenance, and arrange appropriate handling. Do not simply relabel real records as synthetic. Before model evaluation, commission independent expert labels and separate training and holdout sets.
